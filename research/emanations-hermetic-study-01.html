THEORETICAL AND TEXTUAL FOUNDATION

The Greek philosophical Hermetica comprise distinct treatises with varied emphases and transmission histories.

SOURCE ANALYSIS / RESEARCH METHOD

Use Copenhaver and Nock–Festugière; record treatise and section rather than treating the corpus as a single speech.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT AND INTERPRETATION

The philosophical Hermetica survive through Greek manuscripts, Latin translation and excerpts in anthologies. Their dialogues vary in speaker, argument and theological emphasis.

Treat each numbered tractate as an individual witness. The Renaissance collection and its reception are important historical developments but do not prove that the ancient texts formed a single authored book.

REFERENCES & FURTHER READING

Brian Copenhaver, Hermetica (1992); Nock and Festugière, Corpus Hermeticum; Litwa, Hermetica II

Editorial status: expanded interpretive reading guide. References are suggested reading; this is not a critical edition or independently verified translation.
