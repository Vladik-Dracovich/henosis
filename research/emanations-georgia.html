TEXTUAL AND THEOLOGICAL COMMENTARY

Asomtavruli script

Asomtavruli appears in early Georgian Christian inscriptions and manuscripts, including monumental writing. Its letter forms belong to the history of Georgian literacy rather than an independent sacred language.

Distinguish a diplomatic transcription from a normalized edition and from a modern text rendered in historical letters. The latter may be visually appropriate for original literature but does not constitute Old Georgian.

Nuskhuri and manuscript practice

Nuskhuri and Asomtavruli are combined in the ecclesiastical tradition often called Khutsuri. Their functions can differ within the same manuscript, including headings and running text.

Study scribal layout, abbreviations and corrections before interpreting a manuscript's typography as a theological code. Paleographic dating depends on comparative evidence rather than visual antiquity alone.

Early inscriptions

The Bir el Qutt inscriptions near Jerusalem and the Bolnisi Sioni inscriptions are important early witnesses to Georgian Christian literacy. Their contexts connect writing with communities and religious patronage.

A dedication documents a specific historical act and cannot automatically be generalized into a complete account of Georgian religious origins. Record archaeological context alongside linguistic readings.

Old Georgian biblical translation

Old Georgian biblical translations preserve multiple recensions, and their relationships to Greek, Armenian and other textual traditions require passage-specific comparison.

A variant may reflect a different source text, translation strategy or later revision. Compare witnesses before treating a distinctive Georgian expression as evidence for an independent theological doctrine.

Martyrdom of Shushanik

The Martyrdom of Shushanik, attributed to Iakob Tsurtaveli, portrays a conflict involving religious allegiance, family authority and endurance in late antique Caucasia.

Hagiography constructs exemplary conduct through narrative and theological interpretation. Its historical value does not require treating every literary feature as a neutral documentary report.

Life of Grigol Khandzteli

Giorgi Merchule's Life of Grigol Khandzteli describes monastic foundations and religious authority in Tao-Klarjeti. It connects spiritual discipline with institutions, patronage and cultural identity.

Read the work as a hagiographic account of community formation. Its portrayal of ascetic virtue differs in purpose from a philosophical theory of the soul's ascent.

Georgian hymnography

Georgian hymnographic collections preserve translated and original liturgical poetry, with traditions connected to Jerusalem and later monastic centers.

The arrangement of hymns within a liturgical calendar matters as much as their imagery. Compare translation choices and poetic structures before identifying a motif as a distinctively Georgian theological innovation.

Petritsi and Proclus

Ioane Petritsi's translation and commentary on Proclus's Elements of Theology constitute a major witness to Georgian philosophical vocabulary and the reception of Neoplatonism.

Compare Petritsi's Georgian terminology with the Greek propositions and his explanatory commentary. His Christian intellectual setting is essential to understanding the reception rather than simply labeling it pagan survival.

Pre-Christian evidence

Evidence for pre-Christian Georgian religion includes archaeological material and retrospective accounts of conversion, such as traditions concerning Armazi. These sources differ in date and purpose.

Later Christian narratives may frame earlier cults polemically. Avoid reconstructing a detailed ancient Georgian pantheon or liturgy from isolated names and archaeological resemblance alone.

Philological method

Old Georgian differs from modern Georgian in morphology, syntax and vocabulary. Manuscript spelling and editorial normalization introduce additional distinctions.

A reliable edition records the witness, transcription, normalized text and translation separately. Modern Georgian in Asomtavruli should be labeled as modern composition rather than reconstructed ancient language.

PRIMARY TEXTS

Old Georgian biblical and liturgical manuscripts; early hagiography; medieval philosophical translations including Ioane Petritsi’s work on Proclus.

RESEARCH DIRECTION

Distinguish historical Georgian Christian and philosophical literature from reconstructed pre-Christian traditions and modern compositions in Asomtavruli script.

TEXTUAL METHOD

Record the edition, manuscript or inscription identifier, passage number, original language, transliteration and translation before making comparative claims. Mark damaged readings, uncertain restorations and modern adaptations explicitly.
